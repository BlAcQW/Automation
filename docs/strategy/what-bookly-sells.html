Bookly · Positioning brief

What Bookly sells

What a premium appointment business in Accra is actually buying, what has changed since September, and the gaps between what the website says and what is switched on today.

Revised November 2026 (3rd) · written for go-to-market · supersedes the September briefs · product facts checked against the code on 8 November 2026

01The answer in one sentence

You are selling a receptionist for WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger, one that knows the business as well as the owner does.

Not software. Not "AI automation". Not "the WhatsApp Business API". Brand line: Your shop, always open.

The reframe still sets the price anchor, and the new audience makes it stronger.

The customer is not comparing Bookly to booking apps, because they don't use one. They are comparing it to hiring a receptionist, or to the bookings already walking out of the door. For a skin clinic or a private dental practice, one missed GH₵ 850 treatment matters more than a month of the fee. A receptionist costs a salary, takes lunch and goes home at six; Bookly answers at 9 p.m. on a Sunday.

What has changed is the second half of the sentence. In September the assistant knew only services, prices and hours. It now answers from everything the owner writes down about the business, and it remembers returning clients. That is what makes it feel like staff rather than a bot, and it is what a premium business is paying for.

03The nine jobs it does

In the order the client feels them. Status is what is switched on in production today.

  1. Answers while you're working. live On WhatsApp, Instagram and Messenger, in seconds, at any hour. It matches the client's tone, replies in their language, and greets regulars by name.
  2. Knows your business. live Location and parking, payment methods, cancellation policy, how to prepare, aftercare, current offers, the team, common questions. If something isn't written down it says it will check and hands the chat to the owner. It never invents a price or a time.
  3. Only offers times you are really free. live Opening hours, days off, existing bookings, and how many clients the business serves at once.
  4. Takes the deposit before they arrive. live Mobile Money or card, per-service deposit amounts. The slot is held for 30 minutes and released if nobody pays. Today the money goes through the business's own Paystack or Payaza account.
  5. Reminds them. live A confirmation when they book and a reminder one hour before, on WhatsApp or by text from Bookly's own sender, with a link to cancel so the slot can be refilled.
  6. Remembers the regulars. live Past bookings and payment history for verified numbers, and staff notes the assistant uses quietly and never repeats.
  7. Keeps one diary. live Every booking in one list with search, filters and deposit status, and copied into Google Calendar if connected.
  8. Lets the owner step in. live One inbox for all three channels. "Take over" any chat, then hand it back to the assistant.
  9. Protects the client list. live Staff get their own logins and see only the last four digits of a number. Revealing one is recorded.

Job 1 wins the meeting. Jobs 2 and 6 make it feel premium. Jobs 4 and 5 keep them paying in month six.

07Honesty check: where the website is ahead of the product

Fix before selling hard

Payments "through us". The homepage section "You never touch a payment gateway" and the FAQ answer "Do I need a Paystack account? No" describe Bookly collecting the money and the owner withdrawing to Mobile Money. That is built and tested, but it is not switched on: it needs Bookly's own Paystack platform key and Paystack's approval to collect for other businesses. Today a business connects its own Paystack or Payaza account. Either switch it on, or change the copy to "connect your Paystack or Payaza account; we help you set it up".

Fix before selling hard

Reminder timing. The product sends the reminder one hour before the appointment. The homepage example card says "Reminder goes out Wednesday evening", and the launch-video storyboards show "a reminder the day before". Either add a day-before reminder (worth it for premium bookings) or change the copy.

Outside our control, in progress

Meta. Instagram and Messenger are built, but need Meta App Review before other businesses can connect them. WhatsApp for new businesses depends on our business verification and Tech Provider status. This is why sign-up is invite-only.

Know this when asked

Plans. Prices are not public ("coming soon"). The plan limits shown in Settings (services, staff, templates) are display only; just the monthly message quota is enforced.

08The hole in the story

Still the gap worth closing first

The product still cannot prove it works. The home screen shows four counts: today, bookings, open chats, customers. Not one cedi figure for an appointment business.

A single panel changes that:

This month: GH₵ 6,400 in deposits collected · 38 bookings taken while you were with a client · 212 messages answered for you

It justifies any price without arguing, it is the screenshot an owner sends to another owner, and it stops cancellations in month three. It was the top recommendation in September and is still not built.

09What you sell, and what you don't

Not messages. Meta supplies them and bills the business directly on WhatsApp. Instagram and Messenger cost nothing. Say it as a trust line: "You pay Meta's own rates directly. We don't add a cent on top."

What Bookly does supply:

  • Text reminders on Bookly's own sender ("Bookly"), nothing for the owner to set up. live
  • Taking payments for them, with withdrawal to their own Mobile Money. built, not on
  • A WhatsApp number we set up for them, billed in one Bookly bill by MoMo or card. built, not on
